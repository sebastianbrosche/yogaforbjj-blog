Can I Build Muscle With a Herniated Disc? (Yes — Here's How I Did It)

By Sebastian Brosche 2026-06-11 · 8 min read

When the MRI showed my herniated disc, the first thing my doctor said was "no heavy lifting." I interpreted that as "no training at all." For three months, I did nothing. I got weaker. My back hurt more. Then I met a physio who specialized in athletes, and he said the opposite: "You NEED to build muscle. Your spine needs a muscular armor." He was right. I gained 10 pounds of muscle during my disc recovery. Here's exactly how.

Why Muscle Protects Your Discs

Your spine is stabilized by muscles — the deep core (transverse abdominis, multifidus), the glutes, and the back extensors. When these muscles are weak, the load of every movement — including BJJ — transfers directly to your spinal discs and joints. Building muscle around your spine creates a natural weight belt that absorbs and distributes force.

The Safe Training Protocol

Exercises I DID (Safe for Herniated Disc)

  • Dead bugs — core stability without spinal loading
  • Bird dogs — posterior chain + core, zero compression
  • Glute bridges — builds glutes that protect the lower back
  • Side planks — oblique strength without spinal flexion
  • Kettlebell swings — posterior chain power with neutral spine
  • Pull-ups — upper body without spinal compression
  • Farmer carries — core stability under load, no spinal compression
  • Dumbbell Romanian deadlifts — light weight, controlled, posterior chain

Exercises I AVOIDED

  • Barbell back squats — axial loading compresses discs
  • Conventional deadlifts — too much spinal loading during recovery
  • Good mornings — directly loads the lumbar spine
  • Sit-ups/crunches — spinal flexion under load
  • Leg press — compresses the spine against the pad

The 12-Week Muscle-Building Protocol

Week 1-4: Bodyweight only. Dead bugs, glute bridges, bird dogs, side planks. 3 sets of 15. Daily. Focus on mind-muscle connection.

Week 5-8: Add light resistance. Kettlebell swings (12-16kg), farmer carries (16kg per hand), pull-ups (assisted if needed). 3x/week, not daily.

Week 9-12: Progressive overload. Increase weight gradually. Kettlebell swings (20-24kg). Add dumbbell RDLs. Continue core work. You should be noticeably stronger and pain-free at this point.
