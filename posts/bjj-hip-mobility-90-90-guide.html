The 90/90 Stretch: The One Mobility Exercise Every BJJ Player Needs

By Sebastian Brosche 2026-06-10 · 10 min read

I've coached hundreds of grapplers. Every single one — from white belt to black belt — has tight hips. Every. Single. One. And in almost every case, their BJJ injuries — back, knee, groin — trace back to how their hips move. Or rather, how they don't. The 90/90 stretch changed everything for me. It will do the same for you.

Why Your Hips Are the Root of All BJJ Problems

Here's the chain reaction I see in every grappler who walks into my gym with an injury:

Tight hips → Anterior pelvic tilt → Increased lumbar curve → Lower back pain. OR: Tight hips → Limited external rotation → Knee valgus → Meniscus/LCL issues. OR: Tight hips → Reduced abduction → Groin strain.

Three of the most common BJJ injuries. One root cause: tight hips. The 90/90 stretch addresses all of them simultaneously because it works on hip external rotation (front leg) AND internal rotation (back leg) at the same time.

Real talk: I used to think my lower back pain was a back problem. I did every stretch you can imagine for my back. Nothing worked for more than a day. Then I started doing 90/90s daily. After one week, my back pain dropped by 70%. After one month, it was gone. My back wasn't the problem. My hips were.

How to Do the 90/90 Stretch (Proper Form)

Setup

Sit on the floor with your legs in front of you. Bend your right knee to 90 degrees and rotate it outward so your right shin is parallel to the front of your body. Your right knee and ankle should both be on the floor, forming a 90-degree angle. Now place your left leg behind you, also at 90 degrees — your left knee to the side, left shin parallel to the back of your body. Both hip joints should be at 90 degrees.

The Movement

Keep your spine tall — don't collapse forward. Gently lean your torso toward your front shin. Stop when you feel a stretch. Breathe deeply. Hold for 2 minutes. Switch sides.

Common Mistakes

  • Rounding the lower back to reach further — keep the spine long.
  • Not going deep enough — most grapplers need to get their front shin closer to their body.
  • Holding your breath — the stretch happens on the exhale.
  • Bouncing — never bounce. Slow, static holds only.

The Daily Hip Routine (7 Minutes)

Do this every day. No exceptions. It takes 7 minutes.

  1. 90/90 — 2 minutes per side
  2. Pigeon pose — 90 seconds per side (alternative entry to hip external rotation)
  3. Figure 4 stretch (lying down) — 90 seconds per side
  4. Combat base groin stretch — 60 seconds per side
