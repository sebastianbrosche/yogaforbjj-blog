Is 30 Too Old to Start BJJ? (No — And Here's Why)

By Sebastian Brosche 2026-06-11 · 9 min read

I started BJJ at 25. By 28, my back was so bad I couldn't stand up straight. Most people would have quit. I didn't — I adapted. I learned that age isn't the problem. The problem is training like you're 20 when you're not. I'm 40 now and I roll better than I ever did at 25. Here's how.

The Truth About Starting BJJ at 30+

30 is not too old to start BJJ. Neither is 35, 40, or 50. The question isn't whether you CAN start — it's whether you're willing to train SMART.

The grapplers who thrive starting older are the ones who: do mobility work before class, tap early, train at 70% intensity, prioritize recovery, and don't ego-roll. The ones who quit are the ones who try to keep up with 22-year-olds from day one.

Real examples from my gym: Our oldest white belt started at 52. He's been training for 4 years. He doesn't roll with the competition team. He does his mobility work. He taps to everything. And he's having the time of his life. That's BJJ done right.

The Older Beginner's Training Rules

  1. 10 minutes of mobility work before every class. Not optional.
  2. Choose your training partners. Older, calmer, more technical grapplers. Avoid the spazzy white belt.
  3. Tap early. The ego hit lasts 2 seconds. A joint injury lasts months.
  4. Train 2-3x per week. Not more. Your connective tissue needs recovery.
  5. Do mobility work on off days. 10 minutes. Every day.
  6. Sleep 7+ hours. This is when your body repairs.
  7. Eat enough protein. Muscle maintenance is harder after 30.

Is 40 Too Old to Compete in BJJ?

No. Most BJJ tournaments have Masters divisions (30+, 35+, 40+, 45+, 50+). These are some of the most competitive and technical divisions in any tournament. Masters competitors rely on technique over athleticism, which makes their matches fun to watch and safer to compete in.
