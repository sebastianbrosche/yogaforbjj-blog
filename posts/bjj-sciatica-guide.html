BJJ With Sciatica: 5 Triggers to Avoid and 3 Stretches That Actually Help

By Sebastian Brosche 2026-06-10 · 10 min read

The first time I felt it, I thought someone had kicked me in the back of the thigh. I looked around — nobody was there. The second time, I was just lying in bed, turning over. The pain shot from my lower back down to my toes like an electric shock. That was my introduction to BJJ sciatica. It took me six months and three different physios to figure out what was actually causing it.

The BJJ-Specific Cause of Sciatica

Sciatica isn't a condition. It's a symptom — a nerve being compressed or irritated somewhere along its path. In BJJ players, the compression happens in three places:

  1. The lumbar spine (disc pressing on the nerve root) — from being stacked, bridging excessively, or years of guard play.
  2. The piriformis muscle (deep in the glute) — from tight hips and constant external rotation demands.
  3. The hamstring origin (sitting on it in closed guard) — from spending hours with your legs wrapped around a partner.

Most grapplers assume it's the disc. In my experience coaching, it's more often the piriformis or hamstring — which means the treatment is completely different.

5 Training Triggers to Eliminate

  • Getting stacked repeatedly — If someone stacks you, give up the sweep. It's not worth the nerve compression.
  • Sitting in closed guard for extended rounds — Open your guard frequently. Give your hamstring origins a break.
  • Inverting — Berimbolos and shoulder rolls compress the lumbar spine. Skip them during flare-ups.
  • Deep half guard — This position cranks the hip into internal rotation, which can aggravate the piriformis.
  • Butterfly guard with heavy pressure — The flexion + compression pattern is a sciatica trigger for many grapplers.

3 Stretches That Actually Help

1. Piriformis Release (Tennis Ball Technique)

Sit on a tennis ball placed under the belly of your glute (not the bone). Roll slowly until you find the tender spot. Hold for 60 seconds, breathing deeply. This releases the piriformis and takes pressure off the sciatic nerve. Do this after every BJJ session.

2. Nerve Flossing (Slump Test Position)

Sit on the edge of a chair. Slump your spine. Tuck your chin. Extend one leg. Point your toes down. Then slowly point your toes up while looking up. This glides the sciatic nerve through the surrounding tissue and can dramatically reduce symptoms. Repeat 15 times per leg.

3. Supine Figure 4 (Piriformis Stretch)

Lie on your back. Cross one ankle over the opposite knee. Pull the bottom leg toward your chest. Hold 90 seconds per side. This stretches the piriformis without loading the spine.
