Why BJJ Tightens Your Hips

Let me tell you a story. I started BJJ at 38. Before my first class, I could barely touch my toes. After six months of training, my hips felt like they were filled with concrete. My guard was trash, my hip escapes were slow, and my lower back hurt constantly.

Sound familiar? It should. Because tight hips are the most common physical complaint among BJJ practitioners — more common than sore fingers, more common than mat burn, and arguably more damaging than any other mobility issue.

Here's why BJJ wrecks your hips:

  • Guard retention locks you into hip flexion — closed guard, half guard, butterfly — all of them keep your hips bent for extended periods, shortening the hip flexors.
  • Sitting in the shrimp position — every time you shrimp, you're loading one hip in external rotation and the other in internal rotation. Do this 200 times a class and your hips learn to stay there.
  • The modern BJJ posture is hunched — we spend hours in a curled-forward position that pulls the pelvis into posterior tilt, jamming the hip joints.
  • Mount and side control compression — being on the bottom means your hips are pinned in compromised positions for rounds at a time.

Add to this the fact that most of us sit at a desk all day before training, and you've got a recipe for hip catastrophe. Tight hips don't just limit your jiu-jitsu — they're the leading cause of lower back pain in grapplers, knee strain, and even sciatica.

"Every time I see a BJJ player with chronic lower back pain, I check their hips first. Nine times out of ten, tight hips are the root cause. Free up the hips and the back follows."

The good news? Hip mobility is trainable. Your hips aren't "naturally tight." They're tight because you've taught them to be tight through thousands of hours of repetition. And with the right approach, you can teach them to open up.

The 4 Hip Movements Every Grappler Needs

Before we get into the exercises, you need to understand how the hip actually works. The hip is a ball-and-socket joint capable of movement in four primary directions. Most BJJ players are deficient in at least two of them.

1. Hip Flexion

Bringing your thigh toward your chest. You use this in closed guard, pulling guard, and every time you shrimp. The problem? Most BJJ players have too much flexion tone — the hip flexors are chronically shortened and overactive. That's why you feel tightness in the front of your hip when you lie on your back and let your leg hang off the edge of a table.

2. Hip Extension

Moving your thigh behind your body. This is the movement BJJ neglects most. We spend almost zero time in hip extension on the mats. The result? Tight hip flexors and weak glutes — a combination that pulls your pelvis into anterior tilt and crushes your lower back.

3. Hip Internal Rotation

Rotating your thigh bone inward toward the midline. This is critical for passing guard, for knee-on-belly positioning, and for maintaining alignment in side control. Most grapplers have severely limited internal rotation due to tight deep external rotators (piriformis, gemelli, obturators).

4. Hip External Rotation

Rotating your thigh bone outward. The classic "butterfly stretch" position. External rotation is essential for open guard, rubber guard, and for protecting your knees when you're in a compromised position. This is where most of us feel the "hip tightness" we complain about.

If you only do one stretch — say, pigeon pose — you're only addressing one of these four movements. The secret to real hip mobility is hitting all four with targeted exercises. That's exactly what we're about to do.

The 6 Best Hip Mobility Exercises for BJJ

These six exercises form a complete hip mobility toolkit. Do them in order, and you'll cover all four hip movements. Each exercise has numbered steps so you can follow along at home.

🧘 Mobility Drill #1
90/90 Hip Stretch — External & Internal Rotation

The 90/90 is the single most effective hip mobility drill for BJJ. It directly targets the external rotation you lose from closed guard and the internal rotation you lose from years of sitting. See the full 90/90 guide here.

  1. Sit on the floor with both legs bent at 90 degrees. Your front leg should have the knee pointing forward, shin parallel to the front of your mat. Your back leg points behind you, knee bent to 90 degrees, shin parallel to the back of your mat.
  2. Check your alignment: both knees should be at 90 degrees. If your front knee drifts inward or your back hip flares out, adjust until you feel stable contact with the floor.
  3. Sit up tall — don't collapse forward. Imagine a string pulling from the crown of your head toward the ceiling.
  4. Gently lean your torso forward over the front leg. Keep your spine straight. Stop when you feel a stretch in the back hip (external rotation side).
  5. Hold for 5 deep breaths (inhale through nose, exhale through mouth). On each exhale, see if you can sink a millimeter deeper.
  6. Switch sides. Repeat for 3 rounds per side.
⏱ 3 min per side — do daily
🧘 Mobility Drill #2
Couch Stretch — Hip Extension + Hip Flexor Release

The couch stretch is the antidote to years of sitting and hours of guard work. It opens the front of the hip — the hip flexors — and builds the hip extension that BJJ neglects.

  1. Find a couch, a bench, or a sturdy chair. Place it against a wall if needed for stability.
  2. Kneel on the floor facing away from the couch. Place the top of one foot on the couch surface, with your knee on the floor. The other leg should be in a 90-degree lunge position in front of you.
  3. Your back knee should be far enough from the couch that your shin is pressed against the cushion and your thigh is vertical.
  4. Engage your glutes on the back leg — this protects your lower back. Tuck your tailbone slightly (posterior pelvic tilt).
  5. Raise your arms overhead and lean back slightly. You should feel a deep stretch in the front of the back hip and thigh.
  6. Breathe deeply for 5–8 breaths. Do not hold your breath. With each exhale, relax into the stretch.
  7. Switch legs and repeat.
⏱ 2 min per side — alternate days
🧘 Mobility Drill #3
Pigeon Pose — External Rotation + Glute Stretch

Pigeon is the most famous hip stretch in BJJ for a reason. But most people do it wrong. See the full pigeon pose guide for the BJJ-specific modification, but here's the standard version.

  1. Start in a tabletop position (hands and knees).
  2. Bring your right knee forward and place it behind your right wrist. Your right shin should be at an angle — the closer to parallel with the front of your mat, the deeper the stretch.
  3. Slide your left leg straight back behind you, keeping your hips square to the front. The top of your left foot should be on the mat.
  4. Walk your hands forward and lower your torso toward the mat. Keep your hips level — if one hip lifts, place a block or folded towel under it.
  5. Stay for 5–8 breaths, breathing into the back of the front hip (the glute and deep rotators).
  6. To come out, walk hands back, lift your torso, and slide the front knee back to tabletop.
⏱ 2 min per side — do after class or in morning
🧘 Mobility Drill #4
Frog Pose — Hip Abduction + Adductor Release

Frog pose targets the inner thighs and groin — the adductors — which get incredibly tight from guard retention and from clamping your legs on opponents.

  1. Start on all fours. Spread your knees wider than hip-width apart, keeping your shins parallel to each other.
  2. Point your feet outward (dorsiflexed) so the inner edges of your feet press into the mat. This protects your knees.
  3. Walk your hands forward, lowering your chest toward the mat. Keep your elbows on the floor if possible, or use a block under your chest.
  4. Sink your hips back toward your heels slightly. You should feel a stretch deep in your groin and inner thighs.
  5. Do NOT bounce or force the stretch. Breathe and relax for 5–8 breaths.
  6. To exit, walk your hands back under your shoulders and slowly bring your knees together.
⏱ 2 min — do 2–3 times per week
🧘 Mobility Drill #5
Hip CARs (Controlled Articular Rotations) — Full Range of Motion

CARs are not a stretch — they're active mobility. You move the hip joint through its full available range while keeping everything else still. This teaches your brain to access that range under control.

  1. Stand on one leg, holding onto a wall or chair for balance. The other leg is completely relaxed.
  2. Lift the relaxed leg slightly off the floor. Keep your knee straight.
  3. Slowly trace a circle with your big toe — as large a circle as your hip will allow. Move from the hip joint, not the knee.
  4. Go forward for 5 slow circles, then reverse for 5 circles. Each circle should take 5–8 seconds.
  5. Focus on finding the "corners" of your range — the places where you feel restriction. Breathe into those spots.
  6. Switch legs. The goal is to make both sides look identical.
⏱ 2 min total — ideal as a warmup before class
🧘 Mobility Drill #6
Deep Squat Hold — Full Hip Hinge + Ankle Mobility

The deep squat is the most natural human resting position — and the one we've lost from chair living. A deep squat hold opens the hips in flexion and external rotation while also decompressing the lower back.

  1. Stand with feet slightly wider than shoulder-width apart, toes turned out 15–30 degrees.
  2. Lower into a full squat. Go as deep as you can while keeping your heels on the ground.
  3. Press your elbows against the inside of your knees, gently pushing the knees outward.
  4. Keep your chest up. If you can't keep your heels down, place a folded mat or weight plate under them.
  5. Hold the bottom position for 30–60 seconds. Breathe deeply into your belly.
  6. To exit, bring hands to the floor or use your legs to stand back up.
⏱ 1–2 min — great as a cooldown

The Daily 10-Minute Hip Routine

Here's how to put it all together. This routine takes 10 minutes and covers all four hip movements. Do it every day — morning, before class, or as part of your cool-down.

Phase 1: Activate (2 minutes)

  1. Hip CARs — 5 circles each direction, each leg (2 min)

Phase 2: Open (5 minutes)

  1. 90/90 stretch — 3 breaths each side, 2 rounds (2 min)
  2. Couch stretch — 5 breaths each side (2 min)
  3. Deep squat hold — 60 seconds (1 min)

Phase 3: Deep Stretch (3 minutes)

  1. Pigeon pose — 5 breaths each side (2 min)
  2. Frog pose — 5 breaths (1 min)

That's it. Ten minutes. Every day. Do this consistently for 30 days and I guarantee you'll notice a difference in your guard, your hip escapes, and your lower back.

Tracking Your Progress

Hip mobility improves slowly — too slowly to notice day to day. That's why tracking matters. Here are three simple ways to measure your progress.

The Sit Test

Sit cross-legged on the floor. Can you sit up tall with a straight spine? Most BJJ players can't — the hips tilt back and the spine rounds. Over weeks, see if you can sit taller and longer without rounding.

The Squat Test

Record a video of your deep squat from the side. Compare it every two weeks. Look for: do your heels stay down? Is your chest higher? Can you stay longer?

The 90/90 Test

In the 90/90 position, measure how far your torso can lean forward over the front leg. Use a yoga block or ruler. As your external rotation improves, you'll be able to lean further without rounding your spine.

Take photos or videos every two weeks. The visual record will show you progress you can't feel.

Conclusion

Your hips are not broken. They're not "naturally tight." They're adapted to the positions you put them in most — closed guard, sitting at a desk, shrimping, sleeping in the fetal position. The good news is that they can adapt to openness just as easily as they adapted to tightness.

The six exercises in this guide — 90/90, couch stretch, pigeon, frog, hip CARs, and deep squat hold — are the complete toolkit. Do the 10-minute daily routine for 30 days and you'll feel the difference in your guard, your passes, your hip escapes, and most importantly, your lower back.

Your hips are your center of gravity in jiu-jitsu. Everything flows through them. Treat them right and they'll let you train into your 50s, 60s, and beyond.
