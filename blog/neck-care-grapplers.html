Neck Care for Grapplers: Why Your Neck Hurts After BJJ and What To Do About It

June 2026

The BJJ Neck is a Canary in the Coal Mine

Your neck absorbs more punishment in BJJ than almost any other joint. Guillotines, north-south pressure, being stacked, and simply turning your head to avoid getting smashed all stress the cervical spine session after session.

Most grapplers accept neck pain as normal. It is not normal. It is a sign that your neck is being loaded beyond its capacity.

Why the BJJ Neck Gets Destroyed

1. Forward head posture. Most grapplers spend hours looking at screens (phones, computers) and hours training in positions that push their head forward. This creates a forward head posture that loads the cervical spine in a mechanically disadvantaged position.

2. Weak deep neck flexors. Your deep neck flexors (longus capitus and longus colli) are the muscles that stabilise your head. They are almost universally weak in modern humans. When they are weak, your sternocleidomastoid and scalenes take over, creating tension headaches and neck stiffness.

3. Overdeveloped traps. BJJ builds massive upper trapezius muscles. When your upper traps are constantly tight, they compress the cervical spine and limit rotation.

The Daily Neck Protection Protocol

Chin Tucks (Deep Neck Flexor Activation)

Lie on your back with your knees bent. Tuck your chin as if making a double chin. Hold for 5 seconds, relax. Repeat 10 times. This one exercise, done daily, will strengthen your deep neck flexors and change how your neck feels during and after training.

Self-Massage for Upper Traps

Using a lacrosse ball or your fingers, find the knot at the top of your shoulder (the upper trap). Apply moderate pressure and breathe. Hold until the muscle releases, typically 30-90 seconds. Repeat on the other side.

Cervical Rotation

Sit tall and slowly turn your head as far as comfortable to one side. Do not force it. Hold for 30 seconds. Repeat on the other side. This maintains your available range of motion and prevents stiffness from accumulating.

Pre-Roll Neck Prep

Before every session: chin tucks x 10, neck rotations x 5 each side, and shoulder shrugs x 10. This primes your neck for the demands of rolling and reduces the chance of a stinger or spasm mid-session.

When to Be Concerned

Some neck stiffness after hard training is normal. But seek medical attention if you have:

  • Numbness or tingling in your arms or hands
  • Sharp, shooting pain when you move your head
  • Persistent headaches originating from your neck
  • Dizziness or balance issues after rolling

Your neck has to last your entire grappling career. Take care of it starting today.
