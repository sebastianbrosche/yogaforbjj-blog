Shoulder Health for BJJ: How to Keep Your Shoulders Strong and Injury-Free

June 2026

The BJJ Shoulder Problem

Shoulder injuries — specifically AC joint sprains, rotator cuff issues, and labral tears — are among the most common serious injuries in BJJ. The shoulder is inherently unstable (designed for range, not stability), and BJJ loads it in extreme positions: arm bars, kimuras, americana, and posting during takedowns.

The good news is that most shoulder injuries in BJJ are preventable with the right preparation.

The Three Pillars of Shoulder Health

1. External Rotation Range and Strength

BJJ is internally rotation dominant. Guard pulls, frames, and grips all involve internal rotation. Over time, this creates an imbalance: strong, tight internal rotators (pecs, lats) and weak, lengthened external rotators (infraspinatus, teres minor).

The fix: Face pulls with a band or cable — 3 sets of 15, 3-4 times per week. Side-lying external rotation with a light dumbbell — 3 sets of 12 each side. These two exercises directly counter the BJJ internal rotation bias.

2. Scapular Stability

Your shoulder blade (scapula) is the foundation of your shoulder. If your scapula is unstable — if it wings, tilts, or moves inefficiently — your rotator cuff has to work overtime to stabilise the joint.

The fix: YTWL exercises — lying on your stomach, raise your arms to form Y, T, W, and L shapes. 5 reps each, holding for 3 seconds at the top. This builds scapular stability and improves shoulder mechanics.

3. Thoracic Spine Mobility

Your shoulder attaches to your ribcage via your scapula. If your thoracic spine (upper back) is stiff, your scapula cannot move properly, forcing your shoulder joint to compensate.

The fix: Thoracic extension over a foam roller — place the roller under your upper back and let your head and hips drop. 10 slow reps. Also: quadrugen thoracic rotations (cat-cow with rotation) — 10 each side daily.

Pre-Roll Shoulder Prep

  1. Arm circles — 30 seconds forward, 30 seconds backward
  2. Band pull-aparts — 15 reps
  3. Cat-cow with rotation — 5 each side
  4. Deep hanging from a bar — 30 seconds (decompresses the shoulder joint)

Listen to Your Shoulders

Shoulder injuries often start as a vague ache during certain movements. A pinch when reaching behind your back, a click during pressing, or soreness that won't go away after training. These are early warning signs. Address them with the exercises above before they become a full tear.
