The Perfect BJJ Warm-Up: A 10-Minute Pre-Roll Protocol

June 2026

Most BJJ Warm-Ups Are Wrong

The standard BJJ warm-up — jogging in circles, shrimping down the mat, and forward rolls — is better than nothing, but it is not preparing your body for the specific demands of rolling.

A good warm-up does three things: increases blood flow to the tissues you will use, activates the stabilising muscles that protect your joints, and rehearses the movement patterns your sport requires.

Here is a 10-minute pre-roll protocol that does all three. Do this before every session. Your body will thank you.

The 10-Minute Protocol

Minute 0-2: Blood Flow

  • Jumping jacks or high knees — 60 seconds
  • Arm circles forward and backward — 30 seconds each
  • Leg swings forward and side-to-side — 30 seconds each

Minute 2-4: Joint Preparation

  • Deep squat hold (heels down) — 60 seconds
  • Cat-cow stretches — 10 slow reps
  • Neck rotations — 5 each direction
  • Wrist circles and finger flexion/extension — 30 seconds

Minute 4-6: Activation

  • Glute bridges — 10 reps with 3-second hold at top
  • Band pull-aparts — 15 reps
  • Bird-dogs — 8 reps each side (slow, controlled)

Minute 6-8: BJJ-Specific Movement

  • Technical stand-ups — 5 each side
  • Shrimping (slow, emphasising hip extension) — 5 each side
  • Inversion rocks (rock back onto shoulders) — 5 reps

Minute 8-10: Breath and Focus

  • Box breathing: inhale 4, hold 4, exhale 4, hold 4 — 5 rounds
  • Set an intention for the session: speed, technique, survival, or flow

Why This Works

This warm-up addresses the specific joints BJJ stresses: hips, shoulders, neck, and spine. It activates the muscles that stabilise those joints. And it rehearses movements you will actually use during rolling.

You will feel more prepared, move better, and be less likely to get injured in the first round.

Make it non-negotiable.
