BJJ With a Herniated Disc: I Trained Through It. Here's What Worked.

By Sebastian Brosche 2026-06-10 · 12 min read

The MRI showed a 7mm herniation at L5-S1. The radiologist's report said "surgical candidate." The orthopedic surgeon said I should quit BJJ. That was eight years ago. I'm still training, I've never had surgery, and my back is stronger than it was before the injury. This is exactly how I did it.

How I Found Out (And Why You Might Have One Too)

I didn't find out because of a dramatic injury on the mats. I found out because my left foot went numb during a road trip. Couldn't feel the gas pedal. That's when I got the MRI.

Looking back, the signs were there for two years. A twinge here and there. A stiff morning now and then. The occasional shooting pain when I sneezed. Classic disc symptoms that I ignored because they never stopped me from training.

If your BJJ back pain comes with any of these, get an MRI: Numbness or tingling down one leg. Weakness in your foot or calf (difficulty walking on your toes or heels). Loss of bladder or bowel control (this is a medical emergency).

The Protocol That Healed My Disc

This is not medical advice. This is what worked for me and for the dozens of grapplers I've coached through disc issues. Always work with a medical professional.

Phase 1: Acute (Weeks 1-3) — No BJJ

The first 2-3 weeks post-diagnosis are about calming the inflammation and protecting the nerve. No rolling. No drilling. Just walking, gentle decompression, and the exercises below. I know it's hard to take time off. But taking 3 weeks off now can save you from 3 months off later.

  1. Walking: 20 minutes daily. Flat ground. No hills. This pumps fluid through the discs.
  2. McKenzie extensions: Lie on your stomach, prop yourself up on your elbows. Hold 30 seconds. Repeat 5 times. This centralizes disc bulge away from the nerve root.
  3. Deep diaphragmatic breathing: 5 minutes, 3x daily. Activates the parasympathetic nervous system and reduces spinal fluid pressure.
  4. Dead bugs (as described in the lower back pain guide): Begin with just the breathing component if movement hurts.

Phase 2: Rebuilding (Weeks 3-8) — Light BJJ

Once the nerve pain is gone and you can walk without symptoms, you can start easing back into BJJ — with strict rules:

  • Flow rolling only. 50% intensity maximum.
  • Start from the knees. No takedowns.
  • No inversions, no berimbolos, no stack passing.
  • Tap immediately if you feel any nerve tension.
  • 15-minute rounds maximum. No competition training.
  • Do your 10-minute mobility routine before every session.

Phase 3: Full Return (Week 8+) — Sustainable Training

At this point, most grapplers can return to near-normal training. The difference is: you now have a maintenance routine for life.

  • Daily: 10-minute mobility routine (the 5 exercises from the lower back guide).
  • Weekly: 1-2 yoga sessions focused on spine health.
  • Monthly: Check in with your body. Any returning symptoms? Adjust intensity.
  • Yearly: Follow-up MRI if symptoms were severe.

Can You Build Muscle With a Herniated Disc?

Yes. In fact, strengthening the muscles around the spine is essential for disc recovery. I gained 10 pounds of muscle during my recovery because I focused on exercises that didn't compress the spine.

  • SAFE: Dead bugs, bird dogs, side planks, glute bridges, pull-ups, farmer carries.
  • AVOID: Heavy back squats, deadlifts, good mornings, any exercise that loads the spine axially.
  • BEST OPTION: Kettlebell work. Swings train the posterior chain without spinal compression.
