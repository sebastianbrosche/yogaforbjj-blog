Mobility for BJJ: The Grappler's Version (Not Gym Mobility)

Sebastian Brosche 2026-09-13 11 min read

GMB ranks for "mobility for BJJ" with a 10-minute warm-up. Hayabusa ranks with a gear-blog stretch list. Jordan sells a course. Evolve still ranks on stretching with a 2017 pose list. None of them spent 13 years filming grapplers who cannot sit in butterfly without their lower back cheating.

Mobility for BJJ is not "being bendy." It is being able to put a joint where the position requires, under control, while someone is trying to take that position away. Flexibility is the range. Mobility is owning the range. If you only have the first, you get into trouble you cannot get out of.

I built Yoga for BJJ because I wrecked my back doing Judo and then BJJ, and the only thing that let me keep training was this: short, specific mobility, almost every day, in the shapes grappling actually uses.

What mobility for BJJ actually is

Most injuries in jiu jitsu are not about being weak. They are about not having access to the right shape at the right moment: a little out of alignment, unable to find balance under pressure, posting on a knee that had no rotation left.

That is from Do BJJ for Life, principle 6, and it is the whole argument. You do not lose because you skipped curls. You lose (or get hurt) because your hip would not rotate, your thoracic spine would not turn, or your shoulder had no extra degrees when the kimura started.

So a mobility session for BJJ should:

  • Take the joint through its full circle (CARs: controlled articular rotations)
  • Then load a BJJ-relevant shape slowly (90/90, lunge, squat, shoulder pass-through)
  • Then stand up and use the new range in a simple movement (technical stand-up, hip escape, shrimp)
  • Then breathe out longer than you breathe in, so the session does not turn into another fight

If you only lie on a mat and hang, you stretched. If you can shrimp into the range you just opened, you did mobility.

The joints that decide whether you last

You can ignore calves for a month and still train. You cannot ignore these.

Hips. Guard retention, knee cuts, sprawls, standing posture. Train flexion plus external rotation, internal rotation (the neglected one), and extension. Skip the one that feels worst and that is usually where the injury collects.

Thoracic spine and neck. Stacks, back takes, inverted work. If the upper back does not rotate, the neck and lumbar spine take the twist.

Shoulders. Frames, posts, kimura and americana defense. Belt work beats random arm circles.

Knees, as a consequence. The knee is a hinge stuck between hip and ankle. Mobility for BJJ that only "opens the hips" on Instagram and never trains the ankle still dumps torque into the MCL. In a survey of 217 jiu jitsu athletes, nearly 30 percent had suffered a knee injury, most often to the MCL.^1 Most of that is not bad luck. It is a hip and a scramble. Full knee protocol: BJJ with bad knees.

Wrists and big toe. Unsexy. You post on them all night.

A 10-minute mobility for BJJ routine you can do today

No equipment except a belt or towel.

  1. Ankle circles and rocking: 30 seconds each side. You will pass and play guard on these feet.
  2. Hip CARs: 4 slow circles each direction, each hip. Standing, hand on a wall. Do not swing.
  3. 90/90 switches: 8 slow reps. Pause when the trail hip is the stiff one.
  4. Lunge with a reach: 4 each side. Hip flexor plus thoracic rotation, which is a pass and a frame.
  5. Shoulder pass-throughs with a belt: 8 reps, narrowing the grip only if it stays pain-free.
  6. Cat-cow plus thread the needle: 6 cycles. Spine before you get stacked.
  7. Technical stand-up, slow: a few each side. Prove the range is usable.

That is mobility for BJJ as a warm-up. After class, drop the stand-ups and add a 90-second figure-4 or pigeon you can breathe in. Pair it with the BJJ warm-up if class still runs laps.

Off-mat mobility is injury insurance

If the only time you move like a grappler is on the mat, your joints are not prepared for the mat. You sit 22 hours, then you ask your hips to invert. Why you keep getting injured is blunt about this: ten to fifteen minutes a day of floor-based mobility decompresses the spine after sitting, restores hip and shoulder range, and builds control in the positions you roll in.

You brush your teeth so you do not lose them. Brush your joints.

This is also the best active recovery I know. The PEARS recovery blueprint says it clearly: doing a lighter version of your sport is not recovery. It is the same pattern at lower volume. Mobility is contrast. It does not add fatigue if you keep it honest. It gives the joints the ranges drilling never visits.

Strength still matters

Mobility without strength is floppy range. One solid strength session a week (squat, hinge, press, pull) plus the mobility above is enough for most hobbyists. The goal is not a powerlifting total. The goal is tendons and bones that can absorb a bad post. After 30, if you do nothing, you lose that capacity.

Do not ego-lift through back pain. Do not skip the lift because you "only do yoga." I need both. So do you.

Weekly minimum

  • Training days: 8 minutes before class (the routine above or the warmup video)
  • After class: 8 minutes slow (stretching for jiu jitsu)
  • Off days: 10-15 minutes follow-along, hips or full body
  • Once a week: a real strength session

Red flags: sharp groin pain, a knee that twists under load, using the lower back to fake hip range. Stop. Regress. Get assessed if it stays.
