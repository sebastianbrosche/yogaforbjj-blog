Why Am I Not Getting Better at BJJ? The Plateau Isn't a Skill Problem — It's a Body Problem

By Sebastian Brosche 2026-06-10 · 9 min read

You've been training for two years. You know the techniques. You can drill them perfectly. But when you roll, nothing works. Your guard gets passed. You can't retain position. And you have no idea why. I've seen this pattern in hundreds of grapplers. And in almost every case, the problem isn't in your head. It's in your body.

Why Your Guard Gets Passed

You know the technical adjustment: frame, shrimp, recover guard. But when you try it, your shrimp is two inches too short. Your frame collapses under pressure. Why?

Because the hip mobility you need to shrimp effectively is lacking. Because the thoracic extension you need to frame is restricted. Because your core strength runs out after 30 seconds of fighting for guard retention.

I remember hitting this wall as a purple belt. I couldn't understand why my guard retention was terrible even though I knew all the positions. Then I recorded myself rolling and watched. My shrimp was barely moving me. My hips had maybe 50% of the range they needed. I started doing daily hip mobility work. Two months later, my guard retention had completely transformed. My coach asked what I'd changed. "I stopped trying to learn new techniques and started learning how to move better."

The Mobility-Skill Connection

Here's something most BJJ coaches don't talk about: every technique has a mobility prerequisite. The berimbolo requires hip internal rotation that most adults don't have. The truck roll requires thoracic spine extension. The inverted guard requires at least 90 degrees of hip flexion with a neutral spine.

If you're missing the mobility prerequisite, you can drill the technique a thousand times and it will never work in a live roll. Your body simply can't execute the movement.

The 4 Mobility Tests Every BJJ Player Should Pass

  1. The Deep Squat: Can you hold a deep squat with your heels on the ground for 60 seconds? If not, your hip and ankle mobility are limiting your open guard.
  2. The 90/90 Switch: Can you transition from one 90/90 position to the other without using your hands? If not, your hip rotation is insufficient for guard retention.
  3. The Thoracic Extension Test: Lying on your back with a foam roller under your shoulder blades, can you touch your forehead to the floor? If not, your posture is compromising your frames.
  4. The Active Pigeon: In pigeon pose, can you actively bring your front shin closer to your body without rounding your back? If not, your external rotation is limited.

Your New Training Formula

For the next 12 weeks, shift your focus:

  • 80% of your mat time: drilling and rolling (your Jiu-Jitsu).
  • 15% of your mat time: mobility and body prep (your movement quality).
  • 5% of your mat time: reflection and recording (your awareness).

Most grapplers spend 0% on the second and third categories. That's why they plateau. When you do the 15%, everything else gets easier.
