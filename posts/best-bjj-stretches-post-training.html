The Perfect 10-Minute Post-BJJ Stretching Routine

By Sebastian Brosche 2026-06-10 · 8 min read

You just finished a hard roll. Your muscles are warm. Your joints are loose. Your heart rate is elevated. This is the PERFECT time to stretch — and most grapplers walk straight to the locker room. Here's the 10-minute routine I've done after EVERY BJJ session for the last 10 years. It's why I'm still training pain-free at 40.

Why Post-BJJ Is the Best Time to Stretch

After a BJJ session, your muscles are warm (more pliable), your joints are lubricated (synovial fluid has been distributed), and your nervous system is active (better mind-muscle connection). Stretching at this exact moment gives you the most bang for your buck.

Five minutes of stretching post-class is more effective than 30 minutes of stretching cold. This is basic exercise physiology. And yet, 80% of grapplers skip it.

The Routine (10 Minutes, No Equipment)

Minute 0-2: Child's Pose with Side Bends

Kneel on the mat, sit back on your heels, stretch your arms forward. Hold 30 seconds. Walk your hands to the left, hold 30 seconds. Center, then right for 30 seconds. Final 30 seconds back in center with deep breaths. This decompresses the spine after being stacked and twisted.

Minute 2-4: Seated Hamstring + Hip Opener

Sit on the mat. Extend one leg, bring the other foot across to the outside of that knee. Twist toward the bent knee, hook your elbow outside the knee. Hold 60 seconds per side. This targets the hamstrings (which shorten during guard play) and the hips simultaneously.

Minute 4-6: Supine Figure 4 (Piriformis Stretch)

Lie on your back. Cross one ankle over the opposite knee. Pull the bottom leg toward your chest. Hold 90 seconds per side. Your piriformis has been working overtime in every closed guard and half guard position.

Minute 6-8: Spinal Twist

Lie on your back. Bring one knee across your body toward the floor on the opposite side. Keep both shoulders on the ground. Hold 60 seconds per side. Your spine needs to unwind from the rotational loading of the session.

Minute 8-10: Deep Breathing in Corpse Pose

Lie flat on your back, arms at your sides, palms up. Close your eyes. Breathe deeply into your belly for 2 minutes. This activates your parasympathetic nervous system, which is responsible for recovery. The rolling is the stimulus. The breathing is where the healing happens.
