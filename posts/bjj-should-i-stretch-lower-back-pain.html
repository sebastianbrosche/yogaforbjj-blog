Should I Stretch My Lower Back If It Hurts? (Absolutely Not — Here's What To Do Instead)

By Sebastian Brosche 2026-06-11 · 8 min read

I used to stretch my lower back every morning. I'd sit on the floor, reach for my toes, and hold it for 30 seconds, feeling the pull in my spine. And every day, my back hurt more. It took me years to understand that I was stretching the wrong thing — and making my problem worse with every single rep.

Why Stretching Your Lower Back Makes It Worse

Here's the model that changed everything for me: your lower back pain is rarely caused by a "tight" lower back. It's caused by tight hips pulling your pelvis out of position, which forces your lower back to work overtime. When you stretch your lower back directly, you're stretching an area that's already overstretched and unstable.

Think of it like this: your lower back is the middle of a chain. If both ends of the chain (your hips and your upper back) are tight, the middle is being pulled from both sides. Stretching the middle doesn't fix the ends — it makes the middle looser and less able to resist the pull.

What to Do Instead (The 3-Step Lower Back Fix)

Step 1: Release the Hips (Not the Back)

Do the 90/90 stretch. Hold 2 minutes per side. This takes the tension off the pelvis, which takes the tension off the lower back. Do this before you touch anything near your spine.

Step 2: Activate the Core (Take the Load Off Your Back)

Dead bugs. 3 sets of 8 per side. Your deep core (transverse abdominis) acts as a natural weight belt for your spine. When it's firing correctly, your lower back doesn't have to brace against every movement. When it's not firing, your back muscles take the load and eventually spasm.

Step 3: Mobilize the Thoracic Spine (Takes Pressure Off the Lumbar Spine)

Thoracic spine rotations on hands and knees. 10 per side. The thoracic spine is designed to rotate. The lumbar spine is not. If your thoracic spine is stiff, your lumbar spine will compensate by rotating — which it's not built for. Loosening the upper back is one of the fastest ways to reduce lower back pain.

My story: I spent an entire year doing "lower back stretches" — toe touches, seated forward folds, knees to chest. Every single one made my back worse. The day I stopped stretching my back and started freeing my hips, my pain began to disappear. Within two weeks of the 90/90 + dead bug combo, my chronic back pain was 80% gone.

The One Stretch That's Safe for Lower Back Pain

If you absolutely must stretch something near your lower back, do the cat-cow. On all fours, alternate between arching and rounding your spine. This is not a static stretch — it's a mobilization. It pumps fluid through the discs and relieves pressure without overstretching the lumbar muscles.
