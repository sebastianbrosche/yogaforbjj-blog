The BJJ Belt System: A Complete Guide to Rankings, Timeline, and What Each Belt Actually Means

By Sebastian Brosche 2026-06-10 · 13 min read

There's a story about a white belt who asked his professor how long it takes to get a black belt. The professor said, "Ten years." The white belt's face fell. Then the professor said, "But don't focus on that. Just focus on showing up tomorrow." That story contains everything you need to know about BJJ belts. The timeline doesn't matter. The daily practice does. But since everyone asks: here's the complete guide.

The BJJ Belt System Explained

The BJJ belt system is fundamentally different from other martial arts. In karate or taekwondo, belts are earned by memorizing forms and demonstrating techniques. In BJJ, belts are earned by demonstrating effectiveness in live sparring against resisting opponents. This is why BJJ belts carry more weight in the martial arts community — they represent proven skill, not memorized knowledge.

BJJ Belts In Order

The standard BJJ belt order: White → Blue → Purple → Brown → Black. Some schools add stripes (4 stripes per belt) to mark progress between belts. The coral belt (red/black) and red belt are honorary ranks for black belts with decades of contribution to the art.

How Long Does It Take to Get Each BJJ Belt?

These are averages based on consistent training (3-4 sessions per week). Everyone's journey is different.

White Belt: 1-2 years

White belt is survival. The goal is not to learn every technique — it's to learn how to not get submitted immediately, and to build the movement patterns that will support your future game. 50% of white belts quit within the first 6 months. The ones who stay begin to understand Jiu-Jitsu around the 1-year mark.

Blue Belt: 2-4 years

Blue belt is where the real learning begins. You know the basic positions and submissions. Now you need to develop a game. Blue belt is also where most people quit — the grind is real, progress feels slow, and life gets in the way. Blue belt is the filter.

Purple Belt: 2-5 years

Purple belt is where things click. You have a defined game. You can consistently submit blue belts and give black belts competitive rounds. Purple belts are often the best training partners in the gym — skilled enough to push you, safe enough not to injure you.

Brown Belt: 1-3 years

Brown belt is the refinement phase. You are polishing your techniques and addressing weaknesses. Brown belts can teach most classes and often serve as assistant instructors. The gap between brown and black is smaller than the gap between any other belts.

Black Belt: 10-15 years total

The black belt is not the end of the journey — it's the beginning of mastery. Black belt means you understand BJJ well enough to teach others and continue developing your own game for life.

Can You Skip Belts in BJJ?

No. The BJJ belt system doesn't allow belt skipping (unlike judo or other martial arts). Every practitioner must earn each belt in order. There are no "accelerated programs" or "intensive black belt courses." Anyone who offers you a fast track to a BJJ black belt is selling something that doesn't exist.

The exception: Some schools allow talented athletes to receive blue belts faster than average (12-18 months instead of 2 years). But this is acceleration within the system, not belt skipping. And it's controversial — many professors believe there are no shortcuts to mat time.

Can You Get Belts in No-Gi BJJ?

This is increasingly common. Traditional BJJ schools require gi training for belt progression. But many modern no-gi programs have their own ranking systems — usually based on skill level markers rather than colored belts. Some organizations issue belts for no-gi. Some use a different system entirely. If belt progression matters to you, train in a school that offers it.
