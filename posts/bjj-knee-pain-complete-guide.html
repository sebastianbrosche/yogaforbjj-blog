BJJ Knee Pain: The Complete Guide to Protecting Your Knees on the Mats

By Sebastian Brosche 2026-06-10 · 11 min read

It started as a click. Then a catch. Then, one day during a simple knee cut pass, my knee gave out completely. I spent the next four months on the sidelines, watching my teammates roll while I did rehab exercises in the corner. The worst part? It was completely preventable. Here's everything I learned about keeping BJJ knees healthy — the hard way.

Why BJJ Is So Hard on Your Knees

BJJ loads the knee in every plane of motion — flexion, extension, rotation, valgus (bending inward), and varus (bending outward). Most sports load the knee in one or two planes. BJJ loads it in all of them, often under full body weight, and often from unpredictable angles.

The most dangerous positions for BJJ knees: ashi garami (heel hook entries), deep half guard, knee cut passes where the passer drives into a bent knee, and any scramble where your foot gets stuck under a partner's weight.

The hidden factor nobody talks about: Your knee problems probably start in your hips or your ankles. If your ankle dorsiflexion is limited (common from years of wearing BJJ shoes or restrictive footwear), your knee compensates by rotating internally. If your hip internal rotation is limited, your knee takes the rotational load instead.

The 3 Most Common BJJ Knee Problems

1. Meniscus Tears (The "Stuck Knee" Feeling)

Most common in: guard players, especially those who play deep half or inverted guard. The mechanism: rotational load through a bent knee. You shrimp, your foot catches the mat, and your knee twists while bearing weight.

Symptoms: clicking, catching, locking, pain on the inside or outside of the knee joint. Conservative management works for many meniscus tears. Surgery is sometimes needed for mechanical locking.

2. MCL Sprains (The "Knee Cut" Injury)

Most common in: top players who use knee cut passes. The mechanism: your partner drives their knee across your body while your foot is planted and your knee is bent at 90 degrees. The MCL (medial collateral ligament) gets stretched beyond its capacity.

Symptoms: pain on the inside of the knee, swelling, instability when putting weight on that leg. MCL sprains heal well with conservative management in most cases.

3. ACL Injuries (The Scramble Injury)

Most common in: any position where your foot is planted and your body rotates around it. The mechanism: deceleration + rotation with a planted foot — classic non-contact ACL mechanism.

Symptoms: a pop, immediate swelling, inability to bear weight. ACL tears require surgical reconstruction in active grapplers. Recovery is 9-12 months.

The Daily Knee Health Routine (8 Minutes)

Do these every day. They strengthen the muscles around the knee and improve the joint mechanics that prevent injuries.

  1. Ankle CARs (Controlled Articular Rotations): 30 seconds per ankle. Improves ankle mobility so your knee doesn't have to compensate.
  2. Knee CARs: 30 seconds per knee. Active range of motion. Improves proprioception.
  3. Hip CARs: 60 seconds per side. Mobility at the hip reduces rotational stress on the knee.
  4. Single-leg balance: 30 seconds per leg, eyes open, then eyes closed. Improves knee stability under load.
  5. Bodyweight squat: 10 deep reps. Heels on the ground. Full depth. Controls the knee track.
  6. Lateral band walks: 10 steps each direction. Strengthens the glute medius, which controls knee valgus.

How to Modify BJJ Training With Knee Pain

  • Avoid positions that put rotational stress on the injured knee (heel hooks, deep half on that side).
  • Wear a knee brace or compression sleeve for proprioceptive feedback.
  • Avoid sudden directional changes — no explosive shrimping or spinning.
  • Tap early to knee bars, toe holds, and heel hooks on your injured leg.
  • Use a larger training partner as an opportunity to work your top game instead of guard.
