Rehabbing the BJJ Knee: A Grappler's Guide to Coming Back Stronger

June 2026

You Will Probably Hurt Your Knee at Some Point

If you train BJJ long enough, you will deal with a knee issue. It is a statistical certainty. The question is not whether it will happen, but whether you will come back properly when it does.

This guide covers the rehab process for common BJJ knee injuries — MCL sprains, meniscus tears, and ACL recovery — from a grappler's perspective. It is not medical advice. Work with a physiotherapist. But this framework will help you understand what your rehab should look like.

Phase 1: Acute (Week 1-2)

Goal: Reduce inflammation, protect the joint, maintain what you can.

  • Relative rest. Do not roll. You can still train your upper body, do mobility work on your healthy leg, and work on breathing drills.
  • Pain-free range of motion. Gently move your knee through its available range several times per day. This prevents scar tissue from locking the joint.
  • Ice and elevation. First 48-72 hours. After that, heat can help with blood flow.
  • Pain-free isometric work. Quad sets, glute bridges. Do not let your quad atrophy completely.

Phase 2: Regain Range of Motion (Week 2-4)

Goal: Full pain-free range of motion.

  • Heel slides. Lying on your back, slide your heel toward your glutes. Go to the point of discomfort, not pain. Hold for 30 seconds. Repeat 10 times, multiple times per day.
  • Stationary bike. Set the seat high and pedal backward. As range improves, lower the seat and pedal forward. The bike is the single best rehab tool for knee range of motion.
  • Prone knee bends. Lying on your stomach, bend your knee to bring your heel toward your glutes. Use a strap if needed.

Phase 3: Rebuild Strength (Week 4-8)

Goal: Regain strength in the injured leg and address imbalances.

  • Single-leg work. Step-ups, single-leg bridges, single-leg Romanian deadlifts (light). Your injured leg needs to learn to load again.
  • Eccentric hamstring work. Nordic curls or partner hamstring lowers. Hamstring strength is critical for ACL and MCL protection.
  • Calf raises. Both straight-leg and bent-knee variations to target both gastrocnemius and soleus.

Phase 4: Return to Sport (Week 8-12+)

Goal: Simulate BJJ demands in a controlled environment before rolling.

  • Agility work. Lateral shuffles, carioca, single-leg hops, and direction changes. Your knee needs to trust itself under dynamic load.
  • Positional drilling only. Start with a partner who understands your situation. Drill positions where your knee feels safe — side control escapes, mount escapes, technical mount.
  • Flow rolling. 50% intensity, no heel hooks, no deep guard passing for the first 2-3 sessions.
  • Gradual return to full rolling. One round at 70%, then rest and assess. If it feels good, add another round next session.

What to Avoid During Rehab

  • Deep kneeling (kneeling on your heels with knees fully flexed)
  • Heel hooks and knee bars (obviously)
  • Heavy pressure passing that loads the injured knee
  • Returning too early because you "feel fine" walking around

The Mental Side

Coming back from a knee injury is as much mental as physical. Your knee will feel weird for months. It will click, pop, and feel unstable even when it is structurally sound. This is normal.

Trust the process. Trust your rehab. And when you come back, you will have a deeper understanding of your body and a more intelligent training approach.

Most importantly: do not rush. A knee injury that heals properly in 12 weeks is a blip. A knee injury that you re-injure because you came back early can cost you years.
