How Often Should You Train BJJ? (And How Much Is Too Much?)

By Sebastian Brosche 2026-06-11 · 9 min read

When I started BJJ, I trained 6 days a week. I was convinced that more was better. Within 3 months, I had tendinitis in both elbows, a shoulder that clicked every time I raised my arm, and a lower back that hurt when I sat down. I learned the hard way that more isn't better. Better is better.

The Short Answer

For most people: 3-4 sessions per week is the sweet spot. Enough to make consistent progress. Enough recovery time to avoid injury and burnout. Less than 3 and progress is slow. More than 5 and injury risk rises significantly unless you're young, well-recovered, and built for it.

By Experience Level

White Belt: 2-3x/week

Your body is adapting to a sport it's never done before. The connective tissue (ligaments, tendons) takes 6-12 months to strengthen. Training more than 3x/week as a beginner increases injury risk without proportional skill gain.

Blue Belt: 3-4x/week

Your body has adapted. You can handle more volume. 4 sessions is where most blue belts start making real progress.

Purple Belt and Above: 3-5x/week

At purple belt and beyond, you know your body well enough to manage volume. Many advanced grapplers train 4-5x/week but at varying intensities — some hard rounds, some flow rounds.

By Age Group

Under 25: 4-5x/week

Recovery is fast. Connective tissue is resilient. This is the only age bracket where 5+ sessions is sustainable.

25-35: 3-4x/week

Recovery starts to slow. Work and life add stress. 3-4 quality sessions beat 6 mediocre, injury-prone sessions.

35-45: 3x/week

This is the sweet spot for longevity. I'm 40 and I train 3x/week with one competition prep block per year where I bump to 4. My 3 sessions are better quality than my 6 sessions at 25.

45+: 2-3x/week + mobility work

Quality over quantity. Add daily mobility work on off-days. At this stage, consistency over decades beats intensity over months.

Signs You're Training Too Much

  • Your joints hurt in the morning (not muscle soreness — joint pain)
  • You're irritable or depressed without obvious cause
  • Your sleep quality has dropped
  • You're getting sick more often
  • Your BJJ feels 'stuck' or frustrating
  • You don't look forward to training
My rule: If you woke up today and don't want to train, don't train. Take the day off. Do mobility work instead. The mats will be there tomorrow. Your body's willingness to train is the most accurate indicator of whether you need rest.
